Why this is worth five minutes

Clearing things out costs you money. It doesn’t have to.

Every organisation eventually has to move things on — an office refit, a change of stock, the end of a school term. The usual answer is a skip, a disposal fee, and a storeroom that fills up again anyway.

What it costs you today

  • A disposal or haulage fee for things that still work
  • Storage space held by items nobody will use again
  • Staff time arranging collection and paperwork
  • Nothing to show for any of it afterwards

What it costs on Givny

  • Nothing — listing is free, and always will be
  • Someone comes to you and collects it
  • A few minutes per item, from any phone
  • A record of what was diverted, and who it reached

The part most organisations care about

Sustainability and CSR reporting increasingly asks what an organisation diverted from landfill and who benefited. That question is difficult to answer for goods given away informally, because nothing is written down. Everything passed on through Givny is recorded as it happens, so the answer exists before anyone asks for it.

Who lists on Givny

Businesses

Clear stock, fixtures and office equipment — and evidence the diversion for reporting.

NGOs & non-profits

Reach the people you serve, and source goods you would otherwise buy.

Schools & universities

Move on furniture, books and equipment at the end of a term or year.

Churches, mosques & faith groups

Coordinate giving inside your congregation and the streets around it.

Government and public bodies are welcome too — redistributing public assets rather than disposing of them.

What you get

A page of your own, and a team that can run it.

Your own storefront

A public page carrying your logo, your description and everything you have listed — at givny.com/o/your-name. A link you can put in a newsletter, a footer or a tender document.

Your team, not your password

Colleagues get their own logins. Owners, managers and listers each see a different amount, so an intern can add items without touching your account settings. Staff leave; the listings stay yours.

A verified badge

We check that the organisation is real before your page goes public. The badge tells a stranger they are dealing with a registered entity rather than an anonymous account.

Followers who get told

People can follow your organisation. When you list something, they are notified — so a clear-out reaches an audience that already wants what you have, instead of sitting unseen.

An impact record you can actually use

Your dashboard keeps a running count of what you listed, what reached a household, how many separate households you reached, and an estimate of the weight diverted from disposal. It produces a sentence you can paste straight into a report:

“[Your organisation] passed on 34 items to 26 households through Givny, diverting an estimated 410 kg from disposal.”

Weight is an estimate, and the platform says so wherever it appears. It is calculated from the size band recorded against each item, not from weighing — a stated estimate is more useful to a report than no figure at all, and more honest than a false precision.

What we ask in return

Nothing financial. Three commitments, because a page that breaks them damages the people it was meant to help:

  • List things that still work. Broken is fine, if you say so.
  • Answer requests. A page nobody replies from is worse than no page.
  • Hand over when you say you will — a household is planning around it.

The questions we always get

What does it cost?

Nothing. No listing fee, no commission, no subscription. Givny does not take a cut because nothing is sold.

Who collects the items?

The person who asked for it, from an address and at a time you agree with them.

Do we choose who gets it?

Yes. Requests arrive in your inbox and you accept or decline each one. Nothing leaves without your say-so.

What if nobody wants it?

It stays on your page until someone does, or until you remove it. You are never committed to anything you list.

Recognition

Measured on what reached people, not on volume.

Organisations hold a public standing, shown on their storefront and on a leaderboard ranked separately from individual members.

What counts

Items that actually reached a household. How many separate households — ten items to ten homes beats ten to one. Weight diverted. Whether you answer people.

What doesn’t

Listing volume alone. A warehouse posting a thousand items nobody collects ranks below a school that rehomed forty desks.

Why it’s trustworthy

Every figure is calculated from real handovers at the moment it is shown. Nothing is self-reported, and nothing can be edited into existence — including by us.

A crest you can put in a report

Your standing can be downloaded as an image — for a board pack, a sustainability slide, or a post announcing what your team cleared this quarter. A number behind a login does not travel; an image does.

Goods go to households near you, who ask for a specific item and collect it themselves. If an item is claimed and not collected, it goes back on your page.

Organisations are ranked on their own board, separately from individual members.
